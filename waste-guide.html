Household Waste Separation Guide

Simple, effective separation strategies for Kampala households to reduce landfill volume and protect local drainage networks.

🍏
Stream 1: Organic & Wet

Biodegradable Kitchen Waste

Comprises over 75% of domestic rubbish in Kampala. Organic matter rots quickly in our tropical climate, causing strong odours when trapped in plastic sacks.

  • Matooke and cassava peelings
  • Market vegetable and fruit waste
  • Leftover posho, beans, and meat scraps
  • Coffee grounds and tea leaves
  • Garden cuttings and dry foliage
Action Tip: Keep separate in an aerated pail for local backyard composting or community agricultural projects.
♻
Stream 2: Clean Recyclables

Dry Recyclable Materials

Valuable materials that can be diverted from landfills and sold to recycling hubs across the city.

  • Clean mineral water and beverage PET bottles
  • Cardboard boxes and corrugated packaging
  • Aluminium soda cans and food tins
  • Glass condiment jars and beverage bottles
  • Clean HDPE containers (jerrycans, shampoo bottles)
Action Tip: Rinse off grease and food residue before bundling to prevent pest attraction.
⚠
Stream 3: Residual / Hazardous

Residual & Special Handling

Materials that cannot be composted or recycled locally and require municipal disposal.

  • Disposable nappies and sanitary products
  • Contaminated food plastic wrappers (buveera)
  • Dry-cell radio batteries and old electronics
  • Fluorescent tubes and mercury bulbs
  • Expired medical prescriptions
Action Tip: NEVER burn these materials. Open burning of plastics releases hazardous dioxins and particulate matter.

Kampala Household Waste Stream Matrix

Waste Type Proper Home Sorting Collection Method Environmental Impact
Organic / Vegetable Porous bucket or banana-leaf lined basket Bi-weekly municipal pickup or home compost Produces high-yield organic soil conditioner
Plastic Bottles (PET) Crush flat; bundle in breathable woven sack Community recycling drops or collection depots Prevents storm drain blockages in low-lying areas
Paper & Cardboard Keep dry; tie in bundles Collected dry by private concessionaires Reprocessed into domestic carton packaging
Sanitary & Residuals Double-bag in heavy refuse liners Direct sealed concessionaire collection Must go strictly to regulated municipal landfills
