Recycling, Reuse & Circular Economy

Explore community-level plastic drop-off hubs, composting initiatives, and e-waste collection points in Kampala.

The Value in Kampala's Waste

Treating rubbish as a resource transforms urban environmental challenges into economic opportunities for youth and informal waste collectors. When households separate materials at source, downstream contamination drops significantly.

Community Composting: Over 70% of waste can be converted into natural organic manure. Using kitchen peelings for urban bag gardens and suburban compound plots reduces dependence on synthetic fertilisers while cutting city transit haulage costs.

Urban Composting in 4 Easy Steps

  1. Designate a Corner: Choose a well-drained, semi-shaded corner in your backyard or set up an aerated 50-litre container.
  2. Balance Green & Brown: Layer fresh food scraps and matooke peels (green nitrogen-rich) with dry leaves, dry grass, or shredded egg trays (brown carbon-rich).
  3. Moisture & Air: Keep as damp as a wrung-out sponge. Turn every 10–14 days with a garden fork to introduce oxygen.
  4. Harvest: Within 8 to 12 weeks, the material breaks down into dark, earthy, sweet-smelling soil amendment.
