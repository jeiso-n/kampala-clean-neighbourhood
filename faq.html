Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about municipal waste collection, private operators, fees, and city environmental ordinances.

Is this an official website of Kampala Capital City Authority (KCCA)?

No. Kampala Clean Neighbourhood is an independent academic and community innovation prototype created by Victoria University Kampala students for course 1315 ST (Internet Technology & Web Design). It is not an official KCCA or NEMA portal.

Why do some areas receive free collection while others pay monthly fees?

Under the Kampala City Council (Solid Waste Management) Ordinance, KCCA operates a dual framework: directly managed municipal trucks service low-income informal settlements and primary public markets at no charge, while licensed private concessionaires collect from middle-to-high income residences, commercial plazas, and institutions under approved tariff schedules.

What happens when I submit a report on the "Report a Problem" page?

Because this is a static prototype without a commercial backend, reports are saved locally to your browser's localStorage for demonstration purposes. They are not automatically transmitted to KCCA or waste operators.

Is it illegal to burn waste in my compound in Kampala?

Yes. Under the National Environment Act (2019) and city ordinances, open burning of domestic rubbish, dry leaves, and plastics is prohibited within municipal boundaries due to carcinogenic emissions and particulate pollution. Violators are subject to administrative fines from Division Health Inspectors.

How can I verify if a private collection company is officially licensed?

You can check our Waste Collector Directory, which flags licensed concessionaires with formal municipal permits. Never patronise informal tippers who dispose of waste along railway reserves or in wetland corridors.
